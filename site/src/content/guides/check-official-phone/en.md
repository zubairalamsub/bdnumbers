In Bangladesh the same phone model is sold two ways: **official** and **unofficial**. The price gap is often several thousand taka, sometimes more. Before you buy the cheaper one, know what the difference is and how to check that the phone is legal.

## Official and unofficial phones

An **official phone** comes through the brand's Bangladeshi distributor. Its import is registered, it is approved by BTRC, and the brand gives the warranty: if it breaks, the brand's service centres repair it.

An **unofficial phone** is imported by the shop or a trader. It is usually cheaper, but:

- the warranty is the shop's, not the brand's, on the shop's terms;
- it may be a model made for another region, with slightly different network bands or features;
- if its IMEI is not registered in Bangladesh, it may not work on the networks.

So official and unofficial prices should be compared apart. On bdrate.fyi's [mobile prices](/mobile-price) they sit in separate columns, and when a shop doesn't say which a phone is, it reads "not stated".

## What the IMEI is, and where to find it

The IMEI is a phone's own 15-digit identity number. A dual-SIM phone has two.

- Dial **\*#06#** and the phone shows it.
- It is also printed on the box. The two should match; if they don't, be careful.

## Checking it against BTRC's register

BTRC (the Bangladesh Telecommunication Regulatory Commission) keeps a national register of legal handsets, the **National Equipment Identity Register (NEIR)**. To check before you buy:

1. Write a message: **KYD**, a space, then the 15-digit IMEI. For example: `KYD 356789012345678`
2. Send it to **16002**.
3. A reply within a few minutes says whether the number is in the register.

BTRC's [NEIR website](https://neir.btrc.gov.bd) has the check and registration information too, and your operator's customer care can help.

The reply's wording has changed over time; what matters is whether the phone is in the legal register. If it isn't, don't buy it, or ask the seller for a written guarantee.

## What to check when you buy

- **The IMEI on the receipt.** It helps with the warranty, or if the phone is ever lost.
- **Whose warranty it is.** For an "official" phone, ask which service centre repairs it; for an unofficial one, get the shop's warranty period and terms in writing.
- **That the box and phone IMEIs match.**
- **The same model at several shops.** Check that the RAM and storage are the same; the lowest price is usually for the smallest RAM/storage option.
