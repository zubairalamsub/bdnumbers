An EMI (equated monthly instalment) is the same sum you pay every month until a loan is cleared. Part of each payment is interest; the rest pays off the loan. This guide explains the arithmetic only and is not financial advice.

## The formula

On a reducing-balance loan, each month's interest is charged only on what you still owe. The equal instalment that clears it is:

**EMI = P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ ÷ [(1 + r)ⁿ − 1]**

Here **P** is the loan, **r** the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and **n** the number of months. The [EMI calculator](/calculators/emi) uses this formula.

## A worked example

Take Tk 5,00,000 over 3 years (36 months) at 12% a year. The 12% is only an example, not any bank's rate. The monthly rate is 1%, and (1.01)³⁶ ≈ 1.430769.

| | Taka |
|---|---|
| Monthly instalment (EMI) | 16,607.15 |
| Total paid | 5,97,857.58 |
| Total interest | 97,857.58 |

| Month | Interest | Principal | Still owed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5,000.00 | 11,607.15 | 4,88,392.85 |
| 2 | 4,883.93 | 11,723.22 | 4,76,669.63 |

Month 1's interest is 1% of Tk 5,00,000; the rest cuts the loan. As the balance falls, so does the interest: about Tk 164 in the last month. A bank's schedule may differ by a few taka through rounding or day counting. Over 5 years the instalment drops to Tk 11,122.22, but interest rises to Tk 1,67,333.43.

## Why a flat rate looks cheaper

A flat rate charges interest on the whole original loan for the whole term, though you repay some of it every month. Say the same loan is offered at "7% flat":

| | 12% reducing | 7% flat |
|---|---|---|
| Total interest | 97,857.58 | 1,05,000 (5,00,000 × 7% × 3) |
| Monthly instalment | 16,607.15 | 16,805.56 |
| Total paid | 5,97,857.58 | 6,05,000 |

The "7%" loan costs Tk 7,142 more; as a reducing rate it is about 12.8%. On a 3-year loan, a flat rate costs about as much as a reducing rate nearly twice as high, because on average you owe only about half the loan.

## Costs beyond interest

Bangladesh Bank's master circular on bank charges (June 2021) sets these limits for banks:

- **Processing fee:** at most 0.5% of a loan up to Tk 50 lakh, no more than Tk 15,000 (Tk 2,500 on the example); above Tk 50 lakh, 0.3%, up to Tk 20,000. No application fee.
- **Documentation, CIB, stamp, legal and valuation charges:** at actual cost. No service or loan-management fee on top of interest.
- **Early settlement:** at most 0.5% of what is outstanding. After 12 instalments about Tk 3,52,792 is still owed on the example, so at most about Tk 1,764.
- **Late payment:** since 13 May 2026, penal interest on a term loan is capped at 0.5% (it was 1.5%), on the overdue instalment only.

Banks must display their schedule of charges and may not charge anything outside it.

## How lending rates are set now

In June 2023 Bangladesh Bank set the maximum lending rate as a reference rate, SMART (the six-month moving average of Treasury bill rates), plus a margin. On 8 May 2024 it withdrew SMART: rates are now market-based, agreed between bank and customer. Under the rules that remain:

- Each bank declares a rate for each sector, and may go 1% above or below it depending on the customer's risk.
- The sanction letter must say whether the rate is fixed or variable; if variable, how often and by how much it can rise in a year.
- Every bank's declared rates, including consumer credit and housing loans, are on Bangladesh Bank's [lending rate page](https://www.bb.org.bd/en/index.php/financialactivity/interestlending).

## Before you compare offers

- Compare reducing-balance rates, not flat ones.
- Look at the total paid, not just the instalment.
- Add the processing fee and other charges.
- Check whether the rate is fixed or variable.
- Ask about the early settlement fee and part-prepayment.
- Keep the instalment well within your monthly income.
